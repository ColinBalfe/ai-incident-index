What to do · AI voice-clone scams

A loved one calls in a panic, asking for money. What to do right now.

Also called the grandparent scam, the family emergency scam or virtual kidnapping.

Reviewed by Colin Balfe on Oct 27, 2026 · Updated Oct 27, 2026 · Every fact links to its source
If it's happening right now

Hang up and call them back on a number you already have. Scammers can copy a voice from short audio clips, so the voice on the line proves nothing. Don't send money, gift cards or crypto while that call is still going. If your family has a safe word, ask for it.

What to do, step by step

  1. Hang up.Don't argue and don't stay on the line. Keeping you talking is the scam: in one case reported this October, a California mother was kept on the phone for five hours while she wired $5,400.
  2. Call them yourself, on a number you already have.Not the number that called you. If they don't answer, call another relative or a friend who would know where they are.
  3. Ask for your family's safe word.A clone of their voice won't know it. If you don't have one yet, make one today.
  4. Refuse payment by wire transfer, crypto or gift cards.The FTC says requests to pay these ways are signs of a scam. Real emergencies don't need them.
  5. If you already paid, act within the hour.Call your bank or the transfer service and ask them to stop or reverse the payment. Then report it (below) and call your local police.

Warning signs

An unknown or blocked number
Caller ID can also be faked, so a familiar name on screen isn't proof either.
Panic, crying, a crisis
An arrest, an accident, a kidnapping. Fear is designed to stop you thinking.
"Don't hang up" or "don't tell anyone"
Being cut off from everyone who could check the story is the point.
Wire, crypto or gift cards
Money that is fast to send and hard to get back.
The voice is right, but something is off
The FBI says to listen for tone and word choice that don't sound like your loved one.

Protect your family before it happens

  • Agree a family safe word. A word or phrase only your family knows, kept off social media and texts. The FBI recommends it. Make one in two minutes →
  • Limit public clips of your voice. The FBI suggests keeping social media private and only accepting followers you know.
  • Tell the people most likely to be targeted. Parents and grandparents especially. Send them this page.

Send this to your family

Copy it, fill in the blanks, and send it by text or read it to them on the phone.

If you ever get a call from me, or from anyone, saying I'm in trouble and need money, hang up and call me back on my usual number. If you can't reach me, call [another family member]. Ask for our safe word: ________. I will never ask you to pay with gift cards, wire transfers or crypto.

If you already paid or shared something

Speed matters most. What to do depends on how you paid, per the FTC:

You paid or gave…Do this now
Credit cardCall the card issuer right away and ask to reverse the charge.
Debit cardCall your bank or credit union right away and ask for a refund.
Bank transfer, including ZelleCall your bank or credit union right away and ask them to reverse the transfer.
Payment app (Venmo, Cash App, PayPal)Report it in the app right away and ask them to reverse the payment.

Then report it (below). Recovery isn't guaranteed; reporting quickly gives you the best chance.

Where to report it

  • The FTC: ReportFraud.ftc.gov
  • The FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center: ic3.gov
  • Your local police: the non-emergency number, or 911 if someone may really be in danger

Questions people ask

Can AI really copy someone's voice from a short clip?

Yes. The FBI warns that criminals use short audio clips to clone a relative's voice and ask for urgent money or ransom. A voice message, a video on social media or a few words on a call can be enough.

How can I tell if it's really them?

Don't try to judge the voice. Hang up and call them on a number you already have, ask for your family's safe word, or ask something only they would know that isn't online.

What is a family safe word?

A word or phrase agreed in person by your family and never posted or texted. In an emergency call you ask for it; a scammer with a cloned voice won't know it. The FBI recommends one.

I already sent money. What now?

Call your bank or the transfer service immediately and ask them to stop or reverse the payment. Then report it to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov and the FBI at ic3.gov, and tell your local police. Recovery isn't guaranteed, and speed matters.

Where do I report an AI voice scam?

In the US: the FTC (ReportFraud.ftc.gov), the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center (ic3.gov) and your local police. Reporting helps even if no money was lost.

Can caller ID be faked too?

Yes. It's called spoofing: a caller can make a different number, even a familiar one, show on your screen. So caller ID isn't proof. Call back on a number you already have. In the US you can report spoofed calls to the FCC at 1-888-225-5322.
