# Warning Shots: the show

A daily AI briefing of about 13 minutes, plus a 60-second cut for social. It borrows the structure of The President's Daily Brief, the daily intelligence podcast hosted by former CIA officer Mike Baker. That show runs about 21–26 minutes each morning and 10–18 each afternoon, with four stories in a fixed order. We keep its ritual, named segments, sourced facts and spoken confidence, and point every segment at one question: what does this mean for you?

## The clock (about 1,900 words at about 150 words a minute)

| Time | Segment | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | **Cold open** | Music sting. The date, then the headline in one breath, then the hook question. |
| 0:15 | **Ident** (word for word, every day) | "I'm Colin Balfe, and this… is Warning Shots." Music hits. Then the level, said like a weather report: "Today's level is WS-4, Severe, unchanged since yesterday." Weekly, the sourcing line: "Everything in today's show is sourced in public. If we can't link it, we say so." |
| 0:25 | **The menu** | One sentence per segment, with a hook in each. |
| 1:00 | **The Warning** (about 3 min) | The day's biggest risk story in five beats, then three fixed lines (**What we know / What we don't know / What would change this**), then **Our call:** (the labelled assessment), then **What it means for you** and one thing to do today. |
| 4:00 | **Day N** (about 1.5 min) | The running story: its rung (Tried, Proven or Hit), what changed, the next dated test. |
| 5:30 | *(the one ad slot, if there is ever an ad)* | One host-read spot, never before the Warning. |
| 5:45 | **The Breakthrough** (about 2 min) | What AI can newly do, with a number attached, and what's real versus claimed. Not product launches. |
| 7:45 | **Your Life** (about 3 min) | Three quick hits: **your job**, **your money** (usually scams), **your kids**. Each one ends with a takeaway. |
| 10:30 | **The Scoreboard** (about 15 s) | One forecast graded on air, with its original date quoted. Misses read in the same tone as hits. If none came due, say so in one line. |
| 10:45 | **The Watch List** (about 1 min) | The dated tests and events coming up, and what each one would mean. |
| 11:45 | **The Last Shot** (about 45 s) | The reward for finishing: the smaller, odder story, picked for interest, not importance. It is never in the episode title. On a thin day, run a classic from the index ("From the index: case WS-0041, 2024") rather than inflate a weak story. |
| 12:30 | **Sign-off** (word for word) | "That's your Warning Shots for [day, date]. Back tomorrow morning. See what's coming. Know what it means." |

**The 60-second cut:** the cold open, the Warning's headline and its "what it means for you", then the sign-off. It's vertical video with the headline card on screen, for TikTok, Reels and Shorts.

On a quiet day, the segments get shorter, never padded. The clock may run 9–14 minutes; the order never changes.

## What makes the show

Widen the intake, never the lens. A story qualifies if AI is the actor and the consequence lands on ordinary life: your job, your kids, your money, your vote, your safety. Launches, funding, benchmark races, papers and lab politics with no real-world consequence stay out. The promise is "what AI did in the world today, and what it means for you", with the level as the signature and the graded record as the proof.

## Packaging

- **One menu, written once a day.** The morning refresh writes the day's menu once (four signposted lines), and it feeds the episode's menu, the show notes, the site, the social post and, later, the email. That isn't just efficient; for a solo show it's the only way to keep up the cadence.
- **Episode title:** date first, then two hooks, never the Last Shot. For example: "Oct 26: AI Agents Broke Into Anthropic in 12 Hours & A Test Server Fell in 19".
- **YouTube and Shorts:** a single-hook title, no date.

## The five beats of every story

1. **Headline sentence:** the news and the stakes.
2. **Attributed facts:** "According to Gizmodo…", "Anthropic confirms…". Name the source every time.
3. **Plain context:** one line a twelve-year-old would follow.
4. **Caveat:** what isn't known, said out loud.
5. **Kicker:** the tension in one line. "So the real question is…"

## Confidence, spoken

Every claim carries its grade, in words people understand. These lines are the brand:

- "To be clear, …" (what did **not** happen)
- "That's their claim, not an established fact."
- "This one is **Tried**: attempts logged, nobody in." / "**Proven**: a test system fell, no real victim yet." / "**Hit**: a real victim, confirmed."
- "When the estimates range that widely, the evidence is thin."
- "As of this recording, …"

## Our call

Assessment is allowed, but it is always labelled **"Our call:"** and kept to two plain sentences that can be checked later. Fact and judgment never blur. That label is the fix for the snark-as-analysis that makes PDB read as partisan.

## Glossary bank

The same one-sentence explanation every time a term comes up, kept in `posts/podcast/GLOSSARY.md`. Consistency is the point: PDB repeats its explanation of the Bab el-Mandeb strait almost word for word for weeks.

## Voice

- **Short spoken sentences.** Start them with "Now," "But," "So." Repeat the key line once on purpose.
- **Translate every term once.** For example, "PHP, the software under most websites." No acronym or codename carries a headline.
- **One dry aside per story at most, after the fact, never instead of it.** Aim it at hype, spin and press releases, never at a political side. Partisan snark is the most common complaint in PDB's reviews.
- **Use expertise as a lens, not a résumé.** Show it in how a story is read ("the tell here is…"), not in biography.
- **Calendar hooks** connect the show to the listener's day: tax day, Halloween, election week.

## The loop that brings people back

- The same time every day (published by 6:30 AM ET), the same open, the same segment names and the same sign-off.
- **Callbacks:** "As we flagged Friday…" Name the running story's day number every episode.
- **Dated tests:** "On Friday we find out whether…" The answer comes on air, right or wrong.
- **Flash episodes** only for real escalations, such as a confirmed victim or a formal government warning, so the feed is where people check first.
- **One listener call to action**, always the same.

## Never

- Fear without a next step.
- A risk the reporting doesn't support.
- Blurring Tried, Proven and Hit.
- Running past 15 minutes, or padding a quiet day to hit the clock.
- A guest on a weekday.
- Anonymous sourcing, or implying access we don't have. Our authority is the public record, graded and dated. No intelligence-agency styling: no stamps, no redactions, no "classified".
- Sponsors before the ritual is established. When ads come: one slot, labelled in words, and a public list of categories we accept. Nothing that profits from fear of what we cover (no supplements, no prediction markets, no security upsells).
- Paywalling the level, the running story or corrections.
- More than one dry line per episode, and never about a victim.
