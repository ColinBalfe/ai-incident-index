# Warning Shots: the show

A daily 4-minute AI briefing. It borrows the structure of The President's Daily Brief, the daily intelligence podcast hosted by former CIA officer Mike Baker: a fixed ritual, named segments, sourced facts and spoken confidence. It cuts the length to a fifth and points everything at one question: what does this mean for you?

## The clock (about 600 words at about 150 words a minute)

| Time | Segment | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | **Cold open** | Music sting. The date, then the headline in one breath, then the hook question. |
| 0:15 | **Ident** (word for word, every day) | "I'm Colin Balfe, and this… is Warning Shots." Music hits. |
| 0:22 | **The menu** | One sentence per item: "First, … Then, day six of … And in the Last Shot, …" |
| 0:40 | **The Warning** (about 90 s) | The lead story, told in five beats (below). |
| 2:10 | **What it means for you** (about 30 s) | Does it touch the listener, honestly, and **one thing to do today**. |
| 2:40 | **Day N** (about 45 s) | The running story: where it sits on the ladder (Tried, Proven, Hit), what changed, the next dated test. |
| 3:25 | **The Last Shot** (about 25 s) | One strange, striking or darkly funny item to end on. |
| 3:50 | **Sign-off** (word for word) | "That's your Warning Shots for [day, date]. Back tomorrow at 6:37. See what's coming. Know what it means." |

There is no ad before the host speaks. If ads ever come, there is one host-read spot, placed after "What it means for you" and never before the Warning.

## The five beats of every story

1. **Headline sentence:** the news and the stakes.
2. **Attributed facts:** "According to Gizmodo…", "Anthropic confirms…". Name the source every time.
3. **Plain context:** one line a twelve-year-old would follow.
4. **Caveat:** what isn't known, said out loud.
5. **Kicker:** the tension in one line. "So the real question is…"

## Confidence, spoken

Every claim carries its grade, in words people understand. These lines are the brand:

- "To be clear, …" (what did **not** happen)
- "That's their claim, not an established fact."
- "This one is **Tried**: attempts logged, nobody in." / "**Proven**: a test system fell, no real victim yet." / "**Hit**: a real victim, confirmed."
- "When the estimates range that widely, the evidence is thin."
- "As of this recording, …"

## Voice

- **Short spoken sentences.** Start them with "Now," "But," "So." Repeat the key line once on purpose.
- **Translate every term once.** For example, "PHP, the software under most websites." No acronym or codename carries a headline.
- **One dry aside per story at most, after the fact, never instead of it.** Aim it at hype, spin and press releases, never at a political side. Partisan snark is the most common complaint in PDB's reviews.
- **Use expertise as a lens, not a résumé.** Show it in how a story is read ("the tell here is…"), not in biography.
- **Calendar hooks** connect the show to the listener's day: tax day, Halloween, election week.

## The loop that brings people back

- The same time every day (6:37 AM ET), the same open, the same segment names and the same sign-off.
- **Callbacks:** "As we flagged Friday…" Name the running story's day number every episode.
- **Dated tests:** "On Friday we find out whether…" The answer comes on air, right or wrong.
- **Flash episodes** only for real escalations, such as a confirmed victim or a formal government warning, so the feed is where people check first.
- **One listener call to action**, always the same.

## Never

- Fear without a next step.
- A risk the reporting doesn't support.
- Blurring Tried, Proven and Hit.
- Running past 5 minutes.
- A guest on a weekday.
