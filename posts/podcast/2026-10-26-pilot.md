**THE WARNING**
Here's what happened. A months-old startup called Cathedral pointed AI agents at Anthropic, the company behind Claude. According to Gizmodo, citing the Washington Post, those agents found routes into Anthropic's internal systems in under twelve hours.

Anthropic confirms it. It says the agents reached a limited set of its sales, HR and IT systems.

Now, to be clear, Anthropic says no customer chats were touched. No code and no model weights, which are the AI itself. Access was cut the same day. And Anthropic says Cathedral broke the rules of its bug-bounty program, the scheme that pays outsiders to find holes. That part is disputed.

But here's the tell. They didn't get in through the AI. They got in through outside business software, the ordinary tools every company plugs into its back office.

So the real question isn't whether AI companies can be hacked. It's how fast AI can now find the door.

**WHAT IT MEANS FOR YOU**
If you use Claude, Anthropic says your chats weren't touched.

But the way in was the same kind of outside software that your bank, your employer and your kid's school all run. And this is the kind of door now being tested by AI, at machine speed.

One thing to do today: turn on two-step login for your email. It takes about three minutes, and your email is the key to everything else.

**DAY SIX**
Day six of the flaw under most websites, the PHP bugs that AI helped find.

Where it stands: **Proven**. Not Hit. Here's the difference.

A test server, left open on purpose with two thousand dollars in Bitcoin as the prize, fell in nineteen hours, according to Cyber Security News. TU Delft's sensors alone have logged nearly forty-two thousand break-in attempts since the fix shipped.

But, as of this recording, no real victim has been named. Seventy-seven organizations have been named in reporting as attacked, probed or switched off as a precaution. None has confirmed a breach. And there's no formal US government warning yet.

That's the next test. By Friday, will America's cyber agency formally warn about this flaw? We committed to that question in advance, and we'll grade it right here, right or wrong.

If you run a website, ask whoever hosts it one question: are we on a version fixed after October 21st?

**THE LAST SHOT**
And finally: Anthropic filed to go public today. Buried in more than ninety pages of risk factors, the AI company warns that no single insurer, "and potentially the insurance industry in the aggregate", may have the capacity to cover a catastrophic AI incident.

The people building it are telling investors the worst case might be uninsurable. That's not a critic saying it. That's the filing.
