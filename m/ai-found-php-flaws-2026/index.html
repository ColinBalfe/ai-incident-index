AI finds two critical flaws in PHP, the software under about 70% of websites

Wiz; Calif with OpenAI

about 70%of websites run PHP

Three teams independently found two memory-safety flaws in PHP's core using frontier models; PHP shipped emergency fixes on Oct 21, 2026.

How far it has travelled

From a demonstration to real life, in five steps. Empty steps are the point: a demonstration is not a deployment.

Demonstratedshown once, by its developer or a researcher
yes · Oct 21, 2026 · Wiz describes finding the flaws with its own AI agent plus Gemini 4 Argon and GPT-5.6 Sol; Calif reported the same flaw to PHP in August under OpenAI's program. source
Reproducedshown again by someone else
yes · Oct 21, 2026 · Three teams reached the same flaws independently, and attackers reproduced working exploits within days. source
Accessibleavailable to the public or to customers
partial · Oct 21, 2026 · The models are publicly available; the vulnerability-research workflows that found the flaws are not published. source
Reliableworks most of the time on a defined task
partial · Oct 23, 2026 · Forbes, Oct 23: OpenAI's Patch the Planet program (run with Trail of Bits) has found 1,995 bugs, 1,316 of them still awaiting a patch; of 130 AI-written patches deployed, 16 caused functional problems and one a performance problem, none a new weakness. Anthropic's Glasswing has found more than 29,000 possible bugs and triaged about 6,000. No published success rate for discovery of flaws of PHP2Shell's severity. source
Real consequenceshas changed something in the world, for good or ill
yes · Oct 26, 2026 · PHP2Shell: governments from the Netherlands to Japan took public services offline as a precaution; millions of exploit attempts. Unit 42's Oct 23 report of a successful intrusion at a European government body was retracted on Oct 25 (it could not tie the break-in to these flaws); as of Oct 26 code execution is confirmed only on researchers' decoy servers and no organization has confirmed a breach. source

What this could change for you

Government, bank and hospital websites went offline for days as a precaution; the flaw sits under software most people use without knowing it.
