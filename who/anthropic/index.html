Up & Down

  • 2026-10-28 ▲ Up Gave US federal, state, local and tribal governments 40% off many of its services and says about 500 government organizations already use Claude.
    Why it matters: Government seats are sticky: once a state's workflows run on one model, switching costs land on taxpayers, not the vendor. Locking them in while senators question its safety disclosures makes Anthropic harder to regulate against.

    Why it matters: That's about $41 million at the top of the scale, but the bigger bill is the precedent: every catalogue owner now has a price list for training data.

Breakthroughs

  • 2026-10-22 Anthropic says its AI checked the code inside 123 widely used programs and found hundreds of hidden bugs
    Anthropic says its AI mathematically checked the building blocks that many apps and websites rely on, and found defects some of which had gone unnoticed for years. Bugs like these are how hackers get in, so finding them first makes the software you use safer. This is Anthropic's own account so far.
  • 2026-10-27 Anthropic says its AI found 62 serious Chrome flaws, now fixed before attackers used them
    Anthropic says an unreleased model, after initial prompting, found flaws and chained them into working attacks on Chrome, Safari, Android, Windows and Linux; it reported them and all are patched, with no real-world attacks. That is the company's account: The Hacker News found some public flaw records don't clearly match its descriptions. The same skill in the wrong hands is the warning.
