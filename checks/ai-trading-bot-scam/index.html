Is this a scam? · AI trading bot or 'AI-powered' investment platform promising guaranteed returns

An 'AI trading bot' that can't lose. Is it real?

Reviewed by Colin Balfe on Oct 27, 2026 · Updated Oct 27, 2026 · Every fact links to its source
The short answer

A scam if it guarantees returns or a near-perfect win rate: the CFTC says AI can't predict the future or sudden market changes, and the SEC, NASAA and FINRA warn about claims like 'our AI trading system can't lose'.

The check

Answer these honestly. A single "yes" to a warning sign is enough to stop.

  • Does it promise guaranteed returns or a near-perfect win rate?The CFTC lists guaranteed returns and 100 percent 'win' rates as red flags, and says AI can't predict the future or sudden market changes.
  • Is the platform and the person pitching it registered?The SEC, NASAA and FINRA say to check registration before investing, for example with Investor.gov's 'Check Out Your Investment Professional' tool and your state securities regulator.
  • Did it come through a WhatsApp group, social media ad, influencer or stranger?The SEC has charged schemes that used social media ads and WhatsApp 'investment clubs' giving AI-branded tips to steer people to fake trading platforms.
  • Are you offered rewards for recruiting others?The CFTC lists referral bonuses as a red flag. Paying earlier investors with new investors' money is how Ponzi schemes work.
  • Are you asked to pay a fee or tax before withdrawing?The SEC says victims of these schemes were told their accounts were frozen until they paid advance fees. The balance on screen isn't real.
  • How old is the website?The CFTC suggests checking a trading site's domain registration age at lookup.icann.org. A brand-new site is a warning sign.

If it's happening right now

Don't deposit more and don't pay any withdrawal fee or tax. Stop replying, screenshot the platform, chats and wallet addresses, and report it. If you sent money recently, contact your bank, payment app or crypto exchange today.

How this scam works

An ad, influencer, stranger online or WhatsApp 'investment club' promotes an AI trading bot, AI-generated trading signals or an AI-managed platform.

It promises huge or guaranteed returns, needs no experience and only a small first deposit, and may pay you for recruiting friends.

A dashboard shows your balance growing, which persuades you to add more.

The platform may claim to be registered with the SEC, even showing fake certificates or filings.

When you try to withdraw, your account is 'frozen' until you pay fees or taxes, or the site vanishes. Often no trading ever happened.

What AI changes

Here 'AI' is mostly the sales pitch: the SEC alleges one 2024-25 scheme rented out bots supposedly programmed with AI when there were no AI trading bots at all. The SEC, NASAA and FINRA call this kind of AI hype a warning sign, and scammers also use AI to build fake websites and deepfake endorsements.

What to do, step by step

  1. Stop paying.No more deposits, 'upgrades' or release fees. The SEC says victims were asked for advance fees before withdrawals that never came.
  2. Save the evidence.Screenshot the site, app, account balances, chats, names and every payment, including crypto wallet addresses.
  3. If you already paid, act today.Follow the FTC's steps for how you paid: contact your bank, card company, payment app or crypto exchange and say the transaction was fraudulent.
  4. Report it.To the SEC at sec.gov/tcr, the CFTC at cftc.gov/complaint, the FBI at ic3.gov, and your state securities regulator.
  5. Ignore anyone offering to recover your money for a fee.The FBI warns that recovery offers are often a second scam.

Real cases

  • Sep 2026 United States. The SEC sued two pairs of offshore entities, Cryptoaiml and TSAI, alleging they took more than $15 million through AI-generated trading 'signals' and rented-out 'AI' trading bots that didn't exist, with fake platforms, a phony SEC certificate and fees to unfreeze accounts. The cases are pending. (SEC)
  • Dec 2025 United States. The SEC charged three purported crypto trading platforms and four 'investment clubs', including AI Wealth Inc., alleging they used social media ads and WhatsApp groups with AI-generated tips to take at least $14 million from US retail investors. The platforms allegedly never made a trade. (SEC)
  • Mar 2024 United States. In 'AI washing' cases, the SEC settled charges with two investment advisers, Delphia and Global Predictions, for falsely claiming to use AI. They paid $400,000 in total without admitting or denying the findings. (SEC)

Where to report it

  • The SEC: sec.gov/tcr
  • The CFTC (for crypto, forex and commodities trading): cftc.gov/complaint
  • The FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center: ic3.gov (type it in yourself; avoid sponsored search results)
  • Your state securities regulator: find it via NASAA

Questions people ask

Can an AI trading bot really make guaranteed profits?

No. The CFTC says AI can't predict the future or sudden market changes, and promises of guaranteed returns or near-perfect win rates are red flags.

The platform says it's registered with the SEC. Is that enough?

Check it yourself. The SEC alleges some schemes faked an SEC certificate or a filing. Use Investor.gov's 'Check Out Your Investment Professional' tool and contact your state securities regulator.

My account shows big gains. Can I withdraw?

If you're asked to pay a fee or tax first, assume the balance isn't real. In SEC cases, the platforms never executed trades.

What is 'AI washing'?

Claiming to use AI when you don't, or exaggerating what it does, to attract investors. The SEC has charged firms for it, and the SEC, NASAA and FINRA warn that even registered firms shouldn't be trusted if they say AI guarantees strong returns.

Where do I report an AI trading scam?

To the SEC at sec.gov/tcr, the CFTC at cftc.gov/complaint, the FBI at ic3.gov and your state securities regulator.
