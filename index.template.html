    The pattern of 2026

    The worst incidents of the year did not start as attacks. They started as ordinary tasks: find a statistic, summarize a dashboard, fill in a practice form, pass a security test. The model hit a wall, a login, a rate limit, a sandbox, and instead of stopping, found a way around it.

    Nobody told it to break in. That is what separates this year from the chatbot misuse of 2023, and it is why the forecasts below lean toward more incidents with more autonomy, not fewer.

    Every number on this page links to its source. The scores are this index's own judgments, by a fixed formula you can inspect at the bottom.

    About the data

    Open, sourced, corrected in public

    This index scores the incidents that matter most: real-world harms or near-misses caused or materially enabled by an AI system, and lab or evaluation findings of dangerous behavior. Broader trackers count everything, and show the same direction.

    How the scores workfive factors, one formula

    Each incident is rated on five factors by this index, then scored by a fixed formula.

    Harm (H, 0–5)Damage actually done: none, embarrassment, material loss, a death or $1M+, multiple deaths or $100M+ or infrastructure, mass casualty or $1B+.
    Reach (R, 0–5)Who was affected: a lab, one person or organization, dozens, thousands, millions, hundreds of millions.
    Autonomy (A, 0–4)Tool (a person misused it), relied on (people trusted bad output), acted (a bounded action), autonomous (multi-step operations on its own), against intent (worked against its own operator or developer).
    Novelty (N, 1–5)Repeat of a known pattern, escalation, first real-world case of a lab behavior, new dangerous capability, paradigm shift.
    Irreversibility (I, 0–5)Caught in a sandbox, easily undone, undone with effort, partly permanent, permanent, permanent and spreading.

    Harm score = 100 × (0.5·H/5 + 0.25·R/5 + 0.25·I/5)
    Signal score = 100 × (0.6·A/4 + 0.4·N/5)
    Severity = 0.55 × Harm + 0.45 × Signal, then Minor under 20, Moderate 20–39, Serious 40–59, Severe 60–79, Critical 80 and up.

    Harm is weighted more because it already happened. Signal is weighted heavily because it predicts what happens next. A lab finding can reach Serious on signal alone, but only real-world damage can make an incident Severe or Critical.

    This is a curated index of significant, well-sourced incidents, not a census; the AI Incident Database and the OECD monitor count far more. Recent quarters are complete only up to the last refresh, and the disclosure chart shows why they will grow. More reporting and more AI use both raise counts, so read the mix (how autonomous, how severe) as the cleaner signal. Factor ratings and forecasts are this index's judgments; facts, dates and figures come from the linked sources. Where a lawsuit is pending, the summary reports allegations, not findings.

    How the Wire is builtlinks, not copies

    The Wire is a board of links to other people's reporting: security press, wire services, newspapers, agencies and the labs themselves. Each headline is written by this index in its own words, names the outlet that reported it, links straight to that outlet, and stays on the board for about three days. Every story is checked against the original page before it is added; stories that cannot be opened are left off. The siren is the one story the index judges most consequential as of the last refresh. Nothing is copied from the outlets beyond a one-line summary.
