A voice that sounds like your kid is no longer proof it’s your kid.

Voice cloning needs a few seconds of audio, and caller ID can be faked. The defence is older than the technology: a word only your family knows, and one rule — hang up and call back on a number you already have. Make the card, print it or paste it into the group chat, and agree on it before anyone needs it.

Pick something you would never post online: not a pet, a street or a birthday. Two unrelated words work well.

Our family · safe word card

If a call doesn’t feel right, it isn’t. Hang up. Call back.

Our safe word— choose a word —

    Got a suspicious call, text or email right now?

    1. Stop. Don’t pay, don’t read out a code, don’t tap the link. Urgency is the tell.
    2. Call back. Use a number from the back of your card, your statement or the official website, never the one in the message.
    3. Verify the person. Ask for the safe word, or ask something only they would know and a clone could not have learned from the internet.
    4. If money moved, call your bank immediately; the first hours matter most. Then report it: in the US at reportfraud.ftc.gov and, for online crime, ic3.gov (the FBI); elsewhere, your national fraud line.
    5. Tell the family. A scammer who has your relative’s voice will try the others next.

    Why this exists

    The index behind this site records AI voice-clone and deepfake fraud as it happens, alongside everything else AI has done to real people. The protocol above is the standard advice of consumer-protection agencies, written to fit on one card. It is not legal or financial advice, and nothing you type here is stored or sent anywhere.
