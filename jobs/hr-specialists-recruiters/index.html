Can AI do your job? · Human resources specialists and recruiters

Will AI replace HR and recruiters? What the evidence shows

Reviewed by Colin Balfe on Oct 27, 2026 · Updated Oct 27, 2026 · Graded, dated evidence, not predictions
The short answer

HR is one of the first corporate functions where a big company has said plainly that AI replaced staff: IBM cut a few hundred HR roles after its HR chatbot began resolving most routine questions. AI also screens résumés and, in one large experiment, ran first-round interviews well. BLS still projects growth, and AI screening is now the subject of a discrimination lawsuit.

Task by task: what AI can actually do

In real use means deployed at scale; shown in tests or pilots means demonstrated but not yet routine; not yet means we found no credible evidence it does this reliably.

Answering routine employee HR questionsIn real useIBM says its AskHR agent resolves 94% of common questions without a human, automates around 80 HR tasks and handled more than 11.5 million interactions in 2024 (company figures). (IBM, Aug 2025)
Screening and ranking job applicantsIn real useA federal judge let an age-discrimination collective action proceed against Workday, whose AI tools score, sort and rank applicants for employers; Workday says the suit lacks merit. (Holland & Knight, May 2025)
Running first-round job interviewsShown in tests or pilotsIn a field experiment with about 70,000 applicants, AI voice-agent interviews raised job offers 12%, starts 18% and 30-day retention 17%; human recruiters still made every hiring decision. (Marginal Revolution on Jabarian & Henkel, Sep 2025)
Workforce planning and employee relationsNot yetEven IBM's CEO, while predicting routine HR tasks would be fully automated, said HR work such as assessing workforce composition and productivity would be unaffected for about ten years. (Neowin, May 2023)

Jobs evidence so far

Labelled by who says AI was the reason: the company itself, reporters, or a study.

  • 2025-05-07 IBM CEO Arvind Krishna tells the WSJ IBM eliminated a few hundred HR roles using AI, while 'our total employment has actually gone up' (company says AI) (People Matters, May 2025)
  • 2023-05 IBM says it will slow or pause hiring for back-office roles, with HR hit hardest; Krishna: 'I could easily see 30% of that getting replaced by AI and automation over a five-year period' (company says AI) (Neowin, May 2023)
  • 2025-10-28 Amazon cuts about 14,000 corporate jobs; earlier reports said human resources was among targets, and CEO Andy Jassy had written that AI 'will reduce our total corporate workforce' (reported as AI) (NPR via GPB, Oct 2025)

What to do

  1. Move up from transactional HRBenefits questions, verification letters and transfers are the tasks IBM automated first. Employee relations, investigations and workforce planning are holding up.
  2. Learn to audit hiring AIWith the Workday lawsuit proceeding, employers need people who can test screening tools for bias and explain their decisions.
  3. Use AI interviewers carefullyResearch shows AI first-round interviews can work, but humans still make the hire. Own the judgment step.
  4. Consider HR-services firmsBLS expects outsourced HR providers to grow faster than in-house departments.

Questions people ask

Will AI replace HR specialists?

Not on current evidence for the occupation overall. BLS projects 6% growth (+60,100 jobs) for 2025-35 and does not mention AI, but routine HR service roles are being automated at some large employers.

Has any company replaced HR staff with AI?

Yes. IBM's CEO said in 2025 that it eliminated a few hundred HR roles using AI, while hiring elsewhere.

Is AI résumé screening legal?

It is legal but contested. A federal court let an age-discrimination collective action against Workday's AI screening proceed in 2025; the case is ongoing.

Do candidates mind AI interviews?

In one large experiment, 78% of applicants offered the choice picked the AI interviewer, and AI-interviewed candidates got more offers.

How we grade

Each task is graded 'deployed' only when a named organisation reports AI doing it in real work at scale, 'demonstrated' when it works in tests, benchmarks or pilots, and 'not yet' when we found no credible evidence AI does it reliably. For job losses we separate what a company said in its own words ('company says AI') from what reporters or analysts attributed to AI ('reported as AI') and from research ('study'); employment and outlook figures come from the BLS 2025-35 projections.
