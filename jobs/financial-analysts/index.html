Will AI replace financial analysts? The evidence

Reviewed by Colin Balfe on Oct 27, 2026 · Updated Oct 27, 2026 · Graded, dated evidence, not predictions
The short answer

AI tools are in daily use at big banks for finding information and summarising, and the best agents answer about two-thirds of entry-level analyst questions with partial credit. Banks tying job cuts to AI mostly point at operations and back-office roles; we found no bank saying it cut financial analysts specifically, and BLS projects 7% growth.

Task by task: what AI can actually do

In real use means deployed at scale; shown in tests or pilots means demonstrated but not yet routine; not yet means we found no credible evidence it does this reliably.

Jobs evidence so far

Labelled by who says AI was the reason: the company itself, reporters, or a study.

  • 2026-10-23 American Express to cut about 5% of ~77,000 staff; CFO said the aim is to 'automate and take advantage of AI'; roles not specified (company says AI) (International Business Times, Oct 2026)
  • 2026-10-23 Barclays cutting about 5,000 jobs citing 'technology change efficiency driven by AI'; Standard Chartered plans to cut 15%+ of back-office staff by 2030 (company says AI) (AP via Boston 25, Oct 2026)
  • 2025-10-14 Goldman Sachs memo: will 'constrain headcount growth' with a 'limited reduction in roles' as AI drives efficiency, while expecting higher year-end headcount (company says AI) (Fortune, Oct 2025)
  • 2025-05 JPMorgan's Marianne Lake: headcount expected to 'trend down by about 10%' over five years, with operations 'at the tip of the spear' on AI tools (company says AI) (eFinancialCareers, Aug 2026)
  • 2025-01-09 Bloomberg Intelligence: bank tech chiefs expect a net 3% workforce cut over 3-5 years, up to 200,000 jobs, mostly back and middle office (study) (Bloomberg Law, Jan 2025)
  • 2026-10-27 HSBC confirms a consultation in its UK wealth business; the FT, citing unnamed people, reports about half of management and specialist roles and about 70% of financial advisers will go in a push to use AI to serve wealthy clients. What to do

  1. Use agents for the first passLet AI pull figures from filings and draft comps, then check every number; benchmarks show about half of answers fail strict scoring.
  2. Own the judgment and the clientRecommendations, client relationships and explaining risk are where accountability sits, and none of the evidence shows AI taking these on.
  3. Stay out of pure processing rolesBank AI cuts so far target operations and back office; if your role is mostly reconciliation or reporting, build analysis skills.
  4. Watch junior analyst intakesBenchmarks are pitched at 2nd-3rd-year analyst work; changes to graduate classes will show first.

Questions people ask

Will AI replace financial analysts?

Not on current evidence. BLS projects 7% growth to 2035, and top AI agents still fail about half of entry-level analyst questions under strict scoring.

Are banks cutting jobs because of AI?

Several say so in their own words, including Barclays, Standard Chartered, AmEx's CFO, Goldman and JPMorgan, but the roles named are mostly operations and back office, not analysts.

What do analysts use AI for today?

Searching internal research and filings, summarising documents and meetings, and first drafts of analysis that a person checks.

Which analyst tasks are safest?

Forming and defending recommendations, client relationships and judgment calls where someone must be accountable.

How we grade

Each task is graded 'deployed' only when a named organisation reports AI doing it in real work at scale, 'demonstrated' when it works in tests, benchmarks or pilots, and 'not yet' when we found no credible evidence AI does it reliably. For job losses we separate what a company said in its own words ('company says AI') from what reporters or analysts attributed to AI ('reported as AI') and from research ('study'); employment and outlook figures come from the BLS 2025-35 projections.
