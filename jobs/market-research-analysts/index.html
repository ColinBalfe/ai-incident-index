Will AI replace market research analysts? The evidence

Reviewed by Colin Balfe on Oct 27, 2026 · Updated Oct 27, 2026 · Graded, dated evidence, not predictions
The short answer

Market research ranks near the top of Microsoft's list of jobs where AI is most applicable, and about half of researchers already use AI regularly. Tests show AI-generated 'synthetic' respondents can land within a few points on headline numbers but fail at tracking change and subgroups. BLS still projects growth, and we found no company-stated job cuts in this field.

Task by task: what AI can actually do

In real use means deployed at scale; shown in tests or pilots means demonstrated but not yet routine; not yet means we found no credible evidence it does this reliably.

What to do

  1. Use AI for speed, real data for decisionsSynthetic respondents can help with early exploration and hard-to-reach boosts, but tests show they miss changes over time and subgroup differences.
  2. Become the validatorKnowing when AI-coded or synthetic results diverge from real respondents is a skill clients will pay for.
  3. Learn to fine-tune and auditOff-the-shelf models coded survey answers poorly; models trained on your own human-coded data did much better.
  4. Own the business questionDesigning studies and turning findings into decisions draws on context and stakeholder trust that AI tools do not have.

Questions people ask

Will AI replace market research analysts?

Not on current evidence. BLS projects 7% growth (+66,300 jobs) for 2025-35. AI is changing the work, and Microsoft ranks the job among the most AI-applicable, but we found no company-stated cuts.

Are synthetic respondents as good as real ones?

For some headline numbers they come within a few points, but a 2026 test found they tracked change over time no better than a coin toss and broke logical price orders in most cases.

Can AI code open-ended survey answers?

Partly. A 2025 study found general models fell short of human coders; a model fine-tuned on human-coded examples performed acceptably.

How many researchers use AI?

About half use it regularly, according to a 2025 Qualtrics survey of more than 3,000 researchers.

How we grade

Each task is graded 'deployed' only when a named organisation reports AI doing it in real work at scale, 'demonstrated' when it works in tests, benchmarks or pilots, and 'not yet' when we found no credible evidence AI does it reliably. For job losses we separate what a company said in its own words ('company says AI') from what reporters or analysts attributed to AI ('reported as AI') and from research ('study'); employment and outlook figures come from the BLS 2025-35 projections.
