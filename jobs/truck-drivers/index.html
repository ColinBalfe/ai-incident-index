Will AI replace truck drivers? What the evidence shows

Reviewed by Colin Balfe on Oct 27, 2026 · Updated Oct 27, 2026 · Graded, dated evidence, not predictions
The short answer

Driverless trucks are now hauling paying freight on a few highway lanes in Texas and the Southwest, but the leading operator expects about 200 trucks by the end of 2026 against more than 2.2 million truck-driving jobs. Evidence for AI handling city driving, loading, inspections and roadside problems is thin, and BLS still projects driver employment to grow.

What to do

  1. Watch which lanes go driverlessEarly operations are long, predictable interstate runs in Sun Belt states; local, regional and specialised hauling are further off.
  2. Build skills that driverless trucks lackHazmat, oversize loads, cargo securement, customer delivery and equipment troubleshooting all need a person today.
  3. Look at the hub jobsDriverless lanes still need people at terminals for first- and last-mile driving, inspections and recovery.
  4. Track the fleet numbersThe useful signal is how many driverless trucks run without anyone on board, not announcements; about 200 are targeted for end-2026.

Questions people ask

Will AI replace truck drivers?

Not soon at scale. Driverless trucks run on a handful of Southwest highway lanes, with about 200 targeted by end-2026 against 2.2 million driving jobs, and BLS projects 4% growth.

Are driverless trucks on the road now?

Yes. Aurora began commercial driverless runs in Texas in May 2025 and reported nearly 440,000 driverless miles by Jun 2026.

Is there anyone in a 'driverless' truck?

On some trucks, yes: Aurora placed an observer in the cab at PACCAR's request in 2025. The observer does not drive.

Which trucking jobs are safest?

Local delivery, specialised and hazardous loads, and jobs involving loading, securing cargo and customer contact.

How we grade

Each task is graded 'deployed' only when a named organisation reports AI doing it in real work at scale, 'demonstrated' when it works in tests, benchmarks or pilots, and 'not yet' when we found no credible evidence AI does it reliably. For job losses we separate what a company said in its own words ('company says AI') from what reporters or analysts attributed to AI ('reported as AI') and from research ('study'); employment and outlook figures come from the BLS 2025-35 projections.
