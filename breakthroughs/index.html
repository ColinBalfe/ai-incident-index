Breakthroughs: what AI just made possible

The other half of the story. Real advances in medicine, science, weather, the planet and the software we all depend on, written as what they mean for you, with a way to try them when you can. Each carries the same proof label as our warnings: in use now, peer-reviewed, reproduced, or claimed and not yet checked.

  • Claimed, not yet checked Math and physics · Oct 27, 2026

    AI agents checked one of math's biggest proofs, 15,000 pages long, in 24 days

    Axiom says dozens of its AI agents, directed by engineers and mathematicians, turned the classification of finite simple groups into about 43 million lines a computer can check step by step, finding 34 departures from the published proofs, mostly minor. The proof itself was never in doubt. No outside group has rebuilt it yet; Axiom has published the files so others can.

  • In use now Weather · Oct 26, 2026

    AI saw where the hurricane would hit when standard forecasts didn't

    Google's AI called Hurricane Isaias's Florida landfall when the usual physics-based models pointed elsewhere. Better track forecasts mean earlier, better-aimed evacuation warnings. It was weaker than the Hurricane Center's own forecast on how strong the storm would be.

    Try it: Google's Weather Lab shows its AI storm forecasts live (free, Google sign-in) →

  • Claimed, not yet checked Math and physics · Oct 26, 2026

    OpenAI says its AI cracked three problems that stumped mathematicians and physicists for decades

    If the claims hold, AI is starting to do original science, not just summarize it. The math proofs have been checked by computer; leading experts are now reviewing the computing and physics claims in the open.

  • In use now Hospitals · Oct 26, 2026

    AI now helps decide when patients are ready to go home at 12 Virginia hospitals

    Sentara uses an AI tool to watch patients and flag who is ready to leave, across all 12 of its Virginia hospitals. That can free beds and nurses' time. The hospital group says no jobs are cut; its estimate of hours saved is still a forecast.

  • Peer-reviewed Medicine · Oct 22, 2026

    An AI solved 8 in 10 of medicine's hardest diagnosis puzzles. Doctors solved fewer than 4

    On 304 of the toughest published cases, Microsoft's diagnostic AI was right 83% of the time, against 36% for general doctors, in a study in the journal Science. People with rare, hard-to-name illnesses could get answers faster. It is not available to patients yet; a hospital trial comes next.

  • In use now Planet · Oct 22, 2026

    A free map now shows every forest on Earth, down to a 10-metre patch

    Google DeepMind's map sorts the world's forests into six types, such as natural or planted, about 90% accurately, checked at more than 9,000 sites. That makes it far harder to pass off a plantation as old forest, or to hide clearing.

    Try it: The map is free to explore on Google Earth Engine →

  • Claimed, not yet checked Safer software · Oct 22, 2026

    AI checked the code inside 123 widely used programs and found hundreds of hidden bugs

    Anthropic says its AI mathematically checked the building blocks that many apps and websites rely on, and found defects some of which had gone unnoticed for years. Bugs like these are how hackers get in, so finding them first makes the software you use safer. This is Anthropic's own account so far.
