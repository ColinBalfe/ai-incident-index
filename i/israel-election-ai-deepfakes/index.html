Israel's election campaign runs on AI: a minister's deepfake of judges being dragged away, a Netanyahu-shared video evoking a rival's dead son, and bots recycled from the Iran war

Two days before Israel's Oct 27 general election, Al Jazeera (Oct 25) describes a campaign fought with AI-generated video and automated accounts on both sides. On Sep 15 Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich's Religious Zionism party posted a 34-second AI video showing Supreme Court President Isaac Amit, Justice Daphne Barak-Erez and Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara being dragged away from a protest by men in yellow vests; opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot called it 'violent and despicable' and President Isaac Herzog said elections are not fought 'through offensive AI videos', per the Times of Israel. Earlier in the year Netanyahu shared an AI video in which Eisenkot runs toward a figure many took to resemble his son Gal, killed in Gaza, then embraces Ra'am leader Mansour Abbas; his camp said the figure was not meant as the son and swapped it for a female character. Ben-Gvir shared a video portraying Gaza's people as donkeys. Haaretz reported on Oct 22 that dozens of bot accounts built to undermine Iran's government had been redirected to promote Likud and warn voters about Eisenkot and Naftali Bennett. An Open University of Israel monitoring project, Ze Be AI, is reported to have found roughly one in eight election posts AI-generated or altered, 46% of them traced to Likud, which alone produced more than 1,800 AI posts (figures reported by the Sunday Guardian; the project's own publication was not located). The Central Elections Committee restricted Likud's Elector turnout-tracking app on privacy grounds; no platform or regulator action on the deepfakes is reported. Oct 25 update: two days before the vote, Netanyahu's official accounts shared an AI-generated image of a mushroom cloud over a burning city that appeared to be Tel Aviv (the Times of Israel reports the post on X, the Jerusalem Post on Instagram), with text by right-wing activist Yuval Bloomberg warning of 'the danger of a second Holocaust' if voters elect a leader unable to confront Iran, and Netanyahu's own caption that 'the State of Israel is in existential danger'. Gadi Eisenkot replied 'Netanyahu, you're hysterical'; Democrats chairman Yair Golan said 'the prime minister of Israel published an image of an atomic bomb over Tel Aviv' and asked that he 'be kept away from social media'; Naftali Bennett called the claim that Israel can only survive under Netanyahu 'a lie'. Neither outlet says whether the post carried an AI label. Oct 26 update: Alhurra reports an Oct 22 INSS report, drawing on the open-source firm Golden Owl, that linked 157 X profiles posing as Israelis to Iran (located there or using the Iranian version of the app), with profile pictures that were 'stolen or AI-generated', seeding replies under politicians and journalists and contacting activists by WhatsApp; Shin Bet said on Oct 22 it had removed several accounts trying to influence the election, without naming a country. Channel 12 separately reports about 80 Russia-linked fake accounts dealt with by a cybersecurity firm working with Shin Bet; that report does not say AI was used.

Damage

Deepfaked depictions of sitting judges, the attorney general and a bereaved rival, and an AI image of a nuclear strike on Tel Aviv shared by the prime minister, circulated to a national electorate during the campaign; no legal or platform consequence reported
