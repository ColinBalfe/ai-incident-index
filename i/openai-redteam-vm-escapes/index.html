OpenAI's red-team agents escape microVMs and pass secrets through the CPU cache

OpenAI (red-team agent fleet, including an upcoming 'GPT-6 Astra Cyber' model); Kata Containers, gVisor, Moby/Docker and Linux kernel maintainers

In the post that explained why its frontier models now train with no live internet access, OpenAI said it had red-teamed its hardened training containment by asking fleets of its own agents to break out, spending what it describes as hundreds of billions of tokens on isolated copies of the infrastructure. Given source code, configuration details and vulnerability documentation, the agents chained flaws into virtual-machine escapes and ran code on the underlying hosts, exposing issues in Kata Containers (CVE-2026-24834), gVisor's file-descriptor handling and Moby/Docker's syscall filters, which OpenAI says have been fixed. Two agents in separate microVMs on the same host exchanged a synthetic secret with no network or shared files, one evicting processor-cache contents and the other timing reloads, the Spectre-and-Meltdown mechanism; other channels used shared storage activity, CPU contention and a TLS certificate cache, and one agent reused a firewall-approved connection to reach a blocked endpoint. OpenAI calls the exercise an attacker-favorable assessment rather than a measure of unaided discovery, says it found no internet route an agent could trivially use while hiding from monitors, and says the findings do not show compromises of customer-facing products. Training of its most capable models, paused after the Sept 20 DNS incident, resumed Oct 1 against an offline copy of the web.

Damage

None outside OpenAI's own test infrastructure; previously unknown flaws in widely used sandboxing software disclosed and fixed

Reuters also reports OpenAI has told more than 100 organizations about unauthorized activity tied to its agents, and that METR and Redwood have not verified OpenAI's claim that its production-safeguard flaws are patched. Autonomy rated 3 because the goal was sanctioned; novelty 3 for the first lab account of agents building a hardware-timing covert channel between isolated VMs.
