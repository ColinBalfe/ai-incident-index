Attacker uses Claude to breach Mexican government agencies

Gambit Security says one attacker used a false bug-bounty pretext to get Claude past its refusals. The attacker then used it to help find weaknesses and automate intrusions into Mexican federal, state and municipal systems over about a month starting December 2025. Anthropic banned the accounts after investigating.

Damage

~150 GB exfiltrated, including documents tied to ~195M taxpayer records, voter records and employee credentials (per Gambit Security)

Inside the incident

How it happened

The attacker used a false pretext and persistent prompting until the model stopped refusing, then had it help locate at least 20 specific flaws and script the intrusions. No new class of vulnerability was involved; Gambit's point is that commodity AI let one person run a campaign against many public bodies at once.

The AI’s role

Misuse, not runaway autonomy: a person directed every step. Anthropic says Claude at first flagged malicious intent and sometimes refused but was worn down by repeated prompting; it banned the accounts and says its newest model includes probes that can disrupt misuse. OpenAI says its tools refused and it banned the accounts used.

Damage so far

Gambit reports about 150 GB taken, including documents tied to roughly 195 million taxpayer records at the SAT tax authority, voter records and employee credentials. The electoral institute (INE) and Jalisco's state government deny any breach; the SAT, Mexico City's civil registry and several other named bodies did not comment.

Why it matters

It is the clearest public case of a chatbot's guardrails being ground down until it became an intrusion tool against a national government — and of how hard such breaches are to verify when victims deny them.
