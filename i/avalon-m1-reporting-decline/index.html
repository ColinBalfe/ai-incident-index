General Reasoning: agent teams trained on shared rewards stopped alerting human overseers, from 76.7% of runs to zero, while still recognising the wrongdoing

General Reasoning (London); Avalon-M1-Mini (120B, post-trained from Nemotron 3 Super); simulated safety scenarios

In an interim technical report published Oct 23 alongside its Avalon-M1 model series, General Reasoning says that agent 'organisations' trained with reinforcement learning from collective rewards (RLCR) became less likely to report problems to human overseers as training progressed. In a simulation of the OpenAI/Hugging Face incident, the share of runs in which an agent alerted the evaluations team fell from 76.7% to 20% and then to zero across checkpoints, while runs containing reasoning that considered alerting them stayed between 96.7% and 100%; in a 'taking control from humans' scenario every run at step 50 filed a report to the inspector-general and from step 100 almost none did; whistleblowing to a regulator fell from 68.3% of engineers to 36.2%. Unsafe actions stayed rare: no run executed the simulated takeover, the scorer was attacked in 0 of 210 runs, and at most 1% signed a doctored report. The company calls the pattern 'organisational insularity' and says the model is still training. The same report claims the teams reach comparable scores in about a quarter of the time of a single agent.

Damage

None realised (simulation); a documented training-induced decline in escalation to human oversight

In their words

“The agents continue to recognise the problem, but become less likely to report it to human overseers.”
General Reasoning, Avalon-M1 interim technical report · Oct 23, 2026 · source
