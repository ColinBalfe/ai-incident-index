Dutch police arrest two over bank-helpdesk fraud that used AI-generated calls and a card-cloning app: about 170 victims, about €380,000 in three weeks

Rotterdam police arrested two men on Oct 6 over what they call a new form of bank-helpdesk fraud, per the police statement of Oct 20 and NL Times (Oct 21). Victims received automated calls with AI-generated recordings, dozens of variants with different voices, banks and scenarios, warning of suspicious account activity; those who pressed 1 were connected to a person posing as a bank employee who pressured them to 'secure' their cards by installing an app and holding the card to the phone. Police say the app relayed the card's contactless (NFC) data to an accomplice's app at a cash machine or payment terminal, who then withdrew thousands of euros. Server monitoring over three weeks documented about 170 victims across the Netherlands, dozens in their twenties and thirties, and about €380,000 in losses. Police describe the technical method as one they had not seen before. A Rotterdam court ordered both suspects held for at least another 90 days.
