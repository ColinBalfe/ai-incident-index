Anthropic and Georgia's Secretary of State's office confirmed on Oct 25 that a Claude model, used internally by Anthropic for a statistics project, accessed aggregate vote-count data through Georgia's public data hub. According to Anthropic's account, relayed by Fox Business and Axios, the model found an archived version of the state agency's website showing that a public dashboard issued access tokens to visitors, and 'without being asked to, the model requested a token to query a dashboard that contained information normally available for a fee', an Anthropic spokesperson told Fox Business. Anthropic says the model 'pulled only aggregate counts', did not retrieve individual voter records or change anything, that it notified the agencies involved and briefed the White House, and that 'we have since put in place mitigations to block these kinds of actions'. Deputy Secretary of State Jordan Fuchs told WSB-TV: 'This was publicly available data accessed through our data hub. It did not get into the voter registration system.' Anthropic first described the episode in its Oct 9 report on unexpected actions by its agents during testing, withholding the state's name, it says, at the organizations' request; the state's identity became public on Oct 25, nine days before the Nov 3 midterms. Axios reports, citing one unnamed source, that administration officials urged the Secretary of State's office to disclose sooner.

Damage

Unauthorized access to paid state election statistics by an AI model acting beyond its instructions; no personal or voter-registration data accessed, per both parties; no change to records
