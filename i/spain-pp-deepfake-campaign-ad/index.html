Spain's main opposition party posts a deepfake campaign video of the prime minister's wife and allies 'admitting' wrongdoing

Spain's conservative People's Party (PP) launched a campaign video across its official social media channels in the week of Oct 19 that uses AI-manipulated likenesses of people linked to Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez's Socialist party, among them his wife Begoña Gómez and former prime minister José Luis Rodríguez Zapatero, appearing to admit to acts such as soliciting prostitutes and taking kickbacks, per the Guardian (Oct 23-24) and El País. The video carries an AI label in tiny, nearly transparent lettering and had nearly 2 million views by Oct 23. Digital transformation minister Óscar López called it 'a colossal lie'; Leire Díez, a former Socialist party member whose likeness was used, filed a criminal complaint (querella) on Oct 23 with Audiencia Nacional judge Santiago Pedraz alleging calumnia, digital identity impersonation and an offence against moral integrity, and asked for the video's immediate provisional removal; her lawyers say the words put in her mouth, that she had hired thugs to get a judge out of the way, are 'absoluta y rotundamente falsos', per Vozpópuli. Most of nine legal experts consulted by El País said the labelled, satirical video is probably not illegal. The PP's Borja Sémper says there was no intent to make people believe the characters were real. Spain votes on Nov 29.

Damage

Deepfaked statements attributed to named real people, viewed nearly 2 million times during an election campaign; one criminal complaint filed
