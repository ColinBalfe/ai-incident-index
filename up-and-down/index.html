Up & Down: who gained power in AI, who lost it

The people, companies and countries whose fortunes moved, newest first. Each name links to the reporting; the arrow and Why it matters are our read.

  • ▼ Down Oct 29, 2026

    Leading the Future The pro-industry AI super PAC network spent $7.4 million on general-election races by Oct 26, only 31% of it in competitive ones (FiftyPlusOne, via The Intercept); a Data for Progress poll for a rival PAC finds about three in four voters in Iowa, Michigan and Ohio less likely to back a candidate known to have AI super PAC support. The Intercept

    Why it matters: Our read: when a donor's backing costs votes, the money moves to primaries, where a few million dollars can swing a small electorate, and away from November, where it shows. Watch for AI money arriving earlier and quieter in the next cycle.

  • ▼ Down Oct 29, 2026

    xAI Its agents are going door to door offering to buy 418 Southaven, Mississippi homes if owners sign away the right to sue over data-center noise; 18 have agreed, per recordings the Guardian obtained. Futurism (citing The Guardian)

    Why it matters: Buying out the neighbours is cheaper than quieting the turbines, and each signed release shrinks the class in the noise suit. It also tells every town weighing an AI data center what the exit offer looks like once the noise starts.

  • ▲ Up Oct 29, 2026

    Mayo Clinic Its AI caught 73% of pancreatic cancers on CT scans that had been read as clear, against 38.9% for specialist radiologists, a median of 475 days before diagnosis, in a peer-reviewed study in Gut. ScienceDaily (BMJ Group)

    Why it matters: Whoever owns a validated early-warning model sets the screening standard insurers end up paying for. A published result puts Mayo ahead of tech companies selling unreviewed imaging AI to the same hospitals.

  • ▲ Up Oct 28, 2026

    Anthropic Gave US federal, state, local and tribal governments 40% off many of its services and says about 500 government organizations already use Claude. Fox News

    Why it matters: Government seats are sticky: once a state's workflows run on one model, switching costs land on taxpayers, not the vendor. Locking them in while senators question its safety disclosures makes Anthropic harder to regulate against.

  • ▲ Up Oct 27, 2026

    Axiom Math Says its AI agents produced a computer-checked version of a 15,000-page proof in 24 days; no outside group has rebuilt it yet. Runtime Wire

    Why it matters: A machine-checked proof is something a buyer can verify without trusting the seller, which is exactly what AI-written code lacks. If outsiders reproduce it, chipmakers and banks get a reason to pay for proofs of their software, not just tests.

  • ▼ Down Oct 25, 2026

    Anthropic Elvis Presley's rights holders sue over 274 songs, asking $150,000 a track. Rolling Stone (Australia)

    Why it matters: That's about $41 million at the top of the scale, but the bigger bill is the precedent: every catalogue owner now has a price list for training data.

  • ▲ Up Oct 25, 2026

    Samsung and SK hynix Heading for a combined $139 billion in one quarter's profit, on AI memory demand. Korea JoongAng Daily

    Why it matters: Sell shovels in a gold rush: the memory makers are booking the AI profits most of the companies buying AI can't find yet.

  • ▲ Up Oct 25, 2026

    Kazakhstan Joins both the US-led AI bloc and China's, with up to $10 billion of Nvidia data centres on one side and record Chinese investment on the other. ABC News (AP)

    Why it matters: Middle powers are learning the AI race pays whoever won't pick a side, until Washington makes them choose, as it now says they must.

  • ▼ Down Oct 24, 2026

    LiteLLM A master key sat in a public repo since May; an attacker used it to ship a poisoned release that stole AI keys. The Hacker News

    Why it matters: Thousands of products route their AI calls through tools like this. One leaked credential upstream becomes everyone's breach downstream.

  • ▼ Down Oct 23, 2026

    OpenAI and Sam Altman Texas sues both, seeking an injunction to keep minors off ChatGPT. The Texas Tribune

    Why it matters: If one state wins an age gate, every state can ask for one. Checking ages at ChatGPT's scale is the most expensive kind of compliance there is.

  • ▼ Down Oct 23, 2026

    Character.AI 31 states and DC object to the rules for selling it out of bankruptcy. PPC Land

    Why it matters: Whoever buys it now inherits 32 attorneys general and their child-safety demands, and that is priced into every bid. Hearing Oct 30.

  • ▼ Down Oct 23, 2026

    Tesla A car on Full Self-Driving killed a 71-year-old on I-80, police say; the second FSD crash in the area this month. SFGate / Bay City News

    Why it matters: Tesla's valuation leans on autonomy. Each fatal crash on the system shifts who sets the pace, from engineers to regulators.

  • ▼ Down Oct 23, 2026

    Panasonic A staffer put HR data on 334,000 people into a banned AI tool. Kansai TV

    Why it matters: This year's costliest leaks aren't hacks; they're employees with a paste box. A ban without an approved tool next to it doesn't hold.

  • ▲ Up Oct 23, 2026

    Moonshot AI Its Kimi K3.1 Pro jumps to fourth on an independent intelligence ranking, three points behind Claude Opus 5.5; open weights due. Artificial Analysis

    Why it matters: When near-frontier weights are free to download, paid labs lose pricing power, and nobody can recall a model once it's out.
