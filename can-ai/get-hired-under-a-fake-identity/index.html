Can AI help someone pass a job interview under a fake identity?

Reviewed by Colin Balfe on Oct 27, 2026 · Updated Oct 27, 2026 · Graded on a five-step evidence ladder
Yes
The answer

Yes. Fraudsters, most prominently North Korean IT workers, have used AI to doctor ID photos, pass technical interviews and keep jobs they could not do alone.

First shown: Jul 23, 2024. Security firm KnowBe4 disclosed it had hired a North Korean operative who used a stolen US identity and an AI-enhanced stock photo and passed four video interviews. (CyberScoop)

What it means

Remote hiring rests on documents, video interviews and skills tests. AI can help fake all three: altering the photo on a stolen ID, polishing a resume, answering technical questions and, once hired, doing the actual work.

In July 2024 KnowBe4 said a remote engineer it had hired was a North Korean persona using a stolen identity and an AI-enhanced stock photo; the person passed background checks and four video interviews and was caught only when malware appeared on the company laptop. Microsoft reported in June 2025 that North Korean workers use tools such as Faceswap to put their pictures onto stolen documents and have tried voice-changing software. Anthropic reported in August 2025 that operatives who could not write basic code used Claude to pass coding assessments and do the job.

AI is an accelerant, not the whole scheme. These operations also rely on stolen identities, laptop farms and human handlers, and the scheme predates chatbots. How often AI-assisted fakes get through is not measured; Gartner predicts that by 2028 one in four candidate profiles worldwide will be fake.

What it means for you

A video interview and a clean background check no longer prove a remote hire is who they say. The identities used in these schemes are often stolen from real people.

What to do

  1. Verify identity through a trusted stepFor remote roles with system access, check ID documents through a trusted service and consider an in-person or notarised step.
  2. Check where equipment shipsFraudulent hires often ask for laptops to go to an address that does not match their stated home.
  3. Start new hires with limited accessRestrict access for new remote staff until identity and work patterns are confirmed.
  4. Freeze your creditIf your personal data has leaked, a credit freeze makes it harder for someone to use your identity.
