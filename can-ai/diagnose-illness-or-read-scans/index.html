Can AI read medical scans or replace a doctor's diagnosis?

Reviewed by Colin Balfe on Oct 27, 2026 · Updated Oct 27, 2026 · Graded on a five-step evidence ladder
Partly
The answer

Partly. AI reliably helps with specific tasks such as reading certain scans, but no AI is approved to replace a doctor's overall diagnosis, and chatbot health advice has caused harm.

First shown: Apr 11, 2018. The FDA cleared IDx-DR, which screens for diabetic eye disease from retinal images and gives a decision without a clinician interpreting the image. (Review of Optometry)

What it means

AI in medicine mostly means narrow tools: software that looks at an X-ray, CT scan or eye photo and flags a likely problem. The FDA cleared IDx-DR in April 2018 to screen for diabetic eye disease without a specialist reading the image; in its study of 900 patients it caught 87.4% of cases and correctly cleared 89.5% of people without the disease.

There are now many such tools. As of March 30, 2026 the FDA's list held 1,524 AI-enabled devices, 1,163 of them (about 76%) in radiology. In a 2025 study at Penda Health clinics in Kenya, clinicians using a GPT-4o-based assistant made 16% fewer diagnostic errors across 39,849 visits, though patients' own reports of feeling better did not differ significantly.

Whole-case diagnosis is still research. Microsoft's MAI-DxO correctly diagnosed 85.5% of 304 hard published cases versus 20% for physicians working without references, but it is not public or clinically validated.

General chatbots are not medical devices. In 2025 doctors reported a man who developed bromide poisoning after replacing table salt with sodium bromide, saying he acted on ChatGPT's suggestion (the chat log was not available).

What it means for you

AI is increasingly part of how scans are read and how doctors check their thinking, and it can catch mistakes. It is not a substitute for a doctor, and chatbot health advice can be confidently wrong.

What to do

  1. Use chatbots to prepare, not decideAsk AI to explain terms or suggest questions for your doctor, then check the answer with a professional.
  2. Never change medicines or diet on AI advice aloneThe bromide case started with a substitution suggested by a chatbot.
  3. Ask your clinic how AI is usedYou can ask whether an AI tool helped read your scan and whether a clinician reviewed the result.
  4. Get urgent symptoms seen in personChest pain, stroke signs or severe symptoms need emergency care, not a chatbot.

Questions people ask

Is AI better than doctors at diagnosis?

On some research benchmarks AI scores higher than doctors working without references, but those tests are not real clinical practice and the tools are not approved to replace doctors.

How many AI medical devices has the FDA cleared?

1,524 as of March 30, 2026, with about 76% in radiology.

What was the first FDA-cleared autonomous AI diagnostic?

IDx-DR, cleared April 11, 2018, screens for diabetic eye disease without a clinician interpreting the image.

Can I trust ChatGPT for medical advice?

Treat it as a starting point only. OpenAI says its services are not intended for diagnosing or treating health conditions.
