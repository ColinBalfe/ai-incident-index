Can AI fake a real person on a live video call?

Reviewed by Colin Balfe on Oct 27, 2026 · Updated Oct 27, 2026 · Graded on a five-step evidence ladder
Yes
The answer

Yes. Criminals have already run video meetings in which the other faces were deepfakes of real colleagues or officials, and victims paid millions.

First shown: Feb 2, 2024. Hong Kong police disclosed that a finance worker paid HK$200 million (about US$25.6 million) after a video conference in which every other participant, including his CFO, was a deepfake; the firm was later confirmed as Arup. (CNN)

What it means

A live video deepfake swaps or generates a face, and often a voice, in real time so the person on screen appears to be someone else. Unlike a pre-made fake clip, it has to keep up with a conversation.

The landmark case came from Hong Kong. A finance employee at engineering firm Arup was suspicious of an email asking for a secret transaction, then joined a video call with what looked like the UK-based CFO and colleagues. Police said everyone he saw was fake. He sent HK$200 million (about US$25.6 million). In May 2026 Singapore police warned of a similar Zoom scam with deepfaked senior officials that cost a businessman S$4.9 million.

It is not flawless. These scams lean on a pretext (a forged letter, a WhatsApp message, an urgent secret deal) and keep calls short. In June 2025 Microsoft said it had not yet seen North Korean job-fraud operators use combined real-time AI voice and video first hand, though it warned that could come. Glitches and lag still give some fakes away, but they are getting harder to spot.

What it means for you

A familiar face on a video call is no longer proof you are talking to that person. Treat an unexpected request for money or secrets on video the way you would treat one by email.

What to do

  1. Verify on a second channelBefore acting on a money request made on a call, contact the person through a number or chat you already had.
  2. Ask something only they would knowA personal question or agreed code word is harder to fake than a face.
  3. Slow down secret, urgent requestsBuild in a mandatory pause and a second approver for any confidential transfer.
  4. Don't rely on spotting glitchesLag or odd lighting can expose some fakes, but quality is improving; verification beats inspection.

Questions people ask

Has anyone actually lost money to a deepfake video call?

Yes. Arup lost about US$25.6 million in 2024 and a Singapore businessman lost S$4.9 million in 2026, both after calls with deepfaked people.

Can deepfakes fool facial recognition?

Hong Kong police said in 2024 that deepfakes had been used at least 20 times to beat facial recognition in loan and bank-account applications.

Can I spot a live deepfake?

Sometimes: lag, mismatched lighting or glitches around the face. Calling the person back on a known number is the safer test.
