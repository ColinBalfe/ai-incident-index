Can AI detect whether text was written by AI?

Reviewed by Colin Balfe on Oct 27, 2026 · Updated Oct 27, 2026 · Graded on a five-step evidence ladder
No
The answer

No, not reliably. Detectors miss much AI text and wrongly flag human writing, so a detector score should never be treated as proof.

First shown: Jan 31, 2023. OpenAI released a classifier for AI-written text that caught only 26% of AI text and wrongly flagged 9% of human text; it withdrew it on July 20, 2023 for low accuracy. (OpenAI)

What it means

AI-text detectors look for statistical patterns typical of machine writing and give a probability that a passage was AI-written. Schools, publishers and employers use them to check work.

The record is poor. OpenAI's own classifier, released in January 2023, caught about 26% of AI-written text and mislabeled about 9% of human writing; OpenAI withdrew it on July 20, 2023, citing its low rate of accuracy. A Stanford-led study found detectors flagged writing by non-native English speakers as AI-generated while correctly identifying native speakers' writing, and that simple prompting could help AI text evade detection.

Small error rates add up. Vanderbilt University switched off Turnitin's AI detector in August 2023, noting that even the 1% false-positive rate Turnitin claimed would mean about 750 of its 75,000 yearly papers wrongly flagged. Asking a chatbot whether it wrote something is worse still: chatbots cannot recognize their own output.

Watermarks that an AI company embeds when it generates text are a different approach and can work, but only for that company's tools and only if the text is not reworded.

What it means for you

A detector score saying 'AI-written' is a guess, not evidence. Students, especially non-native English writers, can be wrongly accused, and people using AI can slip through.

What to do

  1. Keep your draftsVersion history, notes and earlier drafts are the best evidence that you wrote something yourself.
  2. Ask what the evidence isIf accused, ask which tool was used and point to its published error rates and OpenAI's withdrawal of its own detector.
  3. Teachers: don't rely on a scoreTalk with the student about the work; Vanderbilt and others stopped using detectors for this reason.
  4. Don't ask a chatbotChatbots cannot tell whether they wrote a passage; their answer is not evidence.

Questions people ask

Are AI detectors accurate?

Not reliably. OpenAI's own detector caught about 26% of AI text and flagged 9% of human text before it was withdrawn in 2023.

Why did OpenAI shut down its AI detector?

OpenAI said on July 20, 2023 that the classifier was no longer available due to its low rate of accuracy.

Are detectors biased against non-native English speakers?

A Stanford-led study found GPT detectors flagged non-native English writing as AI-generated while correctly identifying native speakers' writing.

Can ChatGPT tell if it wrote something?

No. Asking a chatbot whether it wrote a text gives an unreliable answer, as the Texas A&M-Commerce case showed.
