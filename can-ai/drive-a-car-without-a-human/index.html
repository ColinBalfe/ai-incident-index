Can AI drive a car without a human?

Reviewed by Colin Balfe on Oct 27, 2026 · Updated Oct 27, 2026 · Graded on a five-step evidence ladder
Yes
The answer

Yes, within mapped service areas. Robotaxis carry paying passengers with nobody at the wheel, but consumer systems such as Tesla's Full Self-Driving still require an attentive driver.

First shown: Oct 8, 2020. Waymo opened its fully driverless ride-hailing service, with no safety driver, to members of the public in metro Phoenix. (Waymo)

What it means

A driverless car uses cameras, radar, lidar and AI software to see the road and decide how to drive. Robotaxi companies limit them to areas they have mapped and tested, and remote staff can help when a car gets stuck.

Waymo opened fully driverless rides to the public in Phoenix in October 2020. By May 2025 it had given 10 million paid rides, and by March 2026 about 500,000 a week across 10 US cities. Its own analysis of more than 270 million driverless miles through June 2026 found 82% fewer injury-causing crashes and 95% fewer serious-injury-or-worse crashes than human drivers in the same cities. That comparison is Waymo's, though it counts crashes regardless of fault.

Failures still happen. A Cruise robotaxi dragged a pedestrian in San Francisco in 2023 and California suspended its permits; Waymo recalled software after its cars illegally passed stopped school buses in Austin (2025) and drove into freeway work zones (2026), and one struck a child near a school in January 2026.

Cars sold to the public are different. Tesla's Full Self-Driving is a driver-assistance system that requires a supervising driver. On Oct 22, 2026, California Highway Patrol said a Tesla in Full Self-Driving mode struck and killed a 71-year-old man crossing an unlit Vallejo freeway at night; whether the system could have avoided him is not established.

What it means for you

In a handful of US cities you can already ride in a car with nobody driving, and the data so far suggest fewer injury crashes than human drivers. But a car you buy with 'self-driving' features still needs you watching the road.

What to do

  1. Know which kind of car you're inA robotaxi with an empty driver's seat is designed to drive itself in its area; Tesla FSD and similar systems require you to supervise at all times.
  2. Stay alert with driver assistanceKeep hands ready and eyes on the road; the system can miss pedestrians, stopped vehicles and road works.
  3. Report robotaxi problemsUse the in-app reporting tool or contact the operator, and report safety issues to NHTSA.

Questions people ask

Are driverless cars safer than human drivers?

Waymo's own data on 270 million-plus miles show 82% fewer injury-causing crashes than human benchmarks in the same cities; independent long-term data are still limited.

Is Tesla Full Self-Driving actually driverless?

No. It is a driver-assistance system that requires an attentive driver, and fatal crashes have occurred with it engaged.

Where can I ride in a robotaxi?

Waymo served 11 US cities by mid-2026, including Phoenix, San Francisco, Los Angeles, Austin, Atlanta and Las Vegas.

When did fully driverless rides start?

Waymo opened fully driverless rides to the public in metro Phoenix on Oct 8, 2020.
