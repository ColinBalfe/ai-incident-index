AI Inc.: the drama, the money and the power plays

The fights, the exits, the filings and the price tags behind AI, and what they mean for the people on the other end. The facts come from the outlet linked on each story; Our read is our analysis, labelled as ours.

  • The markdown Oct 27, 2026

    OpenAI halves the price of an AI it rates 'Critical' for hacking

    OpenAI says GPT-6.1 Terra meets the top 'Critical' cybersecurity level in its own safety framework. Its API costs half what its predecessor did. Help Net Security

    Our read: The same company sets the risk rating and the price. Cheaper access to a top-rated hacking capability widens who can use it, for defence and attack alike: the business logic and the safety logic point in opposite directions.

  • The showdown Oct 26, 2026

    The FTC wants Anthropic's bosses under oath

    The FTC sent 50-page demands to Anthropic and OpenAI for records on security incidents, safeguards, marketing and privacy, and wants testimony from Anthropic executives. It says more AI companies will follow. Axios

    Our read: This landed the same week Anthropic filed to go public. Anything said under oath can end up in front of investors, so expect every lab's safety marketing to be rewritten by lawyers.

  • The fine print Oct 26, 2026

    Anthropic tells investors an AI disaster could be too big to insure

    Its IPO filing says no single insurer, "and potentially the insurance industry in the aggregate", may have the capacity to cover a catastrophic AI incident. The risk factors run past 90 pages. Implicator

    Our read: When the seller says a risk can't be insured, the risk doesn't vanish. It lands on customers, shareholders and, in a big enough event, the public. Watch for insurers writing AI exclusions into business policies.

  • The exit door Oct 26, 2026

    Microsoft offers buyouts to 60,000 people, a quarter of its staff

    VP level and below in the US, Canada, India and the UK, per Bloomberg. Microsoft's memo calls it a new choice and doesn't mention AI. TechCrunch · TechCrunch

    Our read: A buyout shrinks a company without the word 'layoff', and the people with the best options leave first. Apollo's chief economist finds no AI payoff yet outside the biggest tech firms: for most companies, AI is still a cost story.

  • The hangover Oct 26, 2026

    $480 billion gone as China's AI stocks fall 40%

    China's AI-heavy STAR50 index is down 40% since July. Goldman cut its target for Korea's Kospi by 25% as the AI trade unwinds; that index is down more than 35% from June. International Business Times AU · Investing.com

    Our read: The AI boom's first big correction is hitting Asia, where retail buyers are heavily exposed. When chip-earnings expectations reset, the damage travels into savings and pensions, not just tech portfolios.

  • The leak hunt Oct 25, 2026

    OpenAI checked fired safety researchers' phones, Journal reports

    OpenAI security looked for Signal chats with Redwood Research's Ryan Greenblatt, per the Wall Street Journal. The researchers say they were never told what they leaked; Greenblatt calls the accusations false. The Next Web

    Our read: Labs ask the public to trust their safety claims while treating safety staff who talk to outsiders as a leak risk. That tension is what regulators' document demands tend to surface.
